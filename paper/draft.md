# Grid-Constrained, Equity-Aware DC Fast-Charging Accessibility Across Two California Utility Territories

Target venue: IEEE Access. Fallback: IEEE ITEC.

are drafted in subsequent passes, see 01-outline.md.

## Abstract

Equitable access to direct-current fast charging is usually assessed by proximity
to chargers or by station counts per capita. Both miss how nearby supply is shared
among competing demand. We study fast-charging access for disadvantaged communities
across two California utility territories, San Diego County under SDG&E and Orange
and Los Angeles counties under SCE, using a competition-aware accessibility model
from the two-step floating catchment area family. The disadvantaged-community
access gap appears as an imbalance between nearby supply and the higher competing
demand of dense disadvantaged areas, so the pattern is geographic, not categorical.
Measured access is lower for disadvantaged communities in San Diego, ratio 0.725
with 95 percent interval 0.599 to 0.889, though this gap is local and vanishes at a
ten-mile catchment, and in Los Angeles, ratio 0.807 with interval 0.767 to 0.834,
robust across method and radius. It reverses in Orange County, ratio 1.147 with
interval 1.093 to 1.201, where supply scales with that demand. A per-capita supply
diagnostic reproduces the sign of all three ratios, while raw proximity favors
disadvantaged areas everywhere. Renter and multifamily groups are not disadvantaged
in any county, with intervals excluding parity. Coupling the model to multi-utility
distribution-grid hosting capacity, equity-weighted siting closes the San Diego gap
substantially but only partly closes the Los Angeles gap, bounded by within-utility
scarcity and by disadvantaged areas in unassessable LADWP territory. The analysis
is descriptive rather than causal, and the feasibility is screening-level.
