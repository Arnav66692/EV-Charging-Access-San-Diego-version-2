# Mechanism diagnostic, the keystone evidence for Figure 2

Read-and-characterize only. No frozen result was recomputed or changed. The
per-capita figure is a diagnostic proxy for the 2SFCA surface, not the headline
metric. Supply universe is the reconstructed in-scope DCFC set, 969 stations and
6,389 ports, stations with at least one DCFC port inside the three counties.
Catchment is a hard 6-mile cutoff without Gaussian decay or the two-step
normalization, so it tracks the sign and approximate size of the frozen ratios but
is not identical to them.

DAC count and share, in-scope block groups. San Diego 141 of 2,058 (6.9 percent).
Orange 289 of 2,049 (14.1 percent). LA 2,858 of 6,591 (43.4 percent). Orange does
not have the fewest DAC areas. San Diego does.

The per-capita column reproduces the frozen ratios. San Diego 0.34/0.46 = 0.74
(frozen 0.725). LA 0.28/0.38 = 0.74 (frozen 0.807). Orange 0.46/0.46 = parity
(frozen 1.147, the distance-decayed two-step tips Orange just above parity).

Verdict. The reversal is geographic, not a method artifact, but not because Orange
DACs are uniquely close to chargers. DAC areas are charger-adjacent and dense in
all three counties, and raw proximity favors DAC areas everywhere, so proximity
cannot explain the sign flip. What separates the counties is whether nearby supply
scales with the higher demand density of DAC areas. In Orange it does, ports scale
with population and per-capita supply is at parity. In San Diego and LA it does
not, DAC catchments hold far more competing population but not proportionally more
ports, so per-capita supply falls to about three-quarters of the non-DAC level.

north-central core, Santa Ana and Anaheim, into Buena Park and Fullerton. LA,
across the basin, central and South LA, Long Beach, Compton, the San Fernando
Valley around Van Nuys.
